Pharmaceutical Technology I — Syrups

Formulation, viscosity builders, preparation methods and sweeteners for pharmaceutical syrups — organised for quick review, practice and recall.

Key Rules & Facts

The essentials only — not the full lecture. Use this as your last-minute review sheet.

Self-preservation rule

An artificial syrup needs sweetness (artificial sweetener), viscosity (viscosity builder), and self-preservation — reached at a 60% concentration.

Viscosity builder families

Glycogenic vs. non-glycogenic. Non-glycogenic splits into natural (tragacanth, acacia), semi-synthetic (Na CMC, HPMC), and polyols (sorbitol, glycerin).

Three incompatibilities

  • Anionic polymers ✗ cationic drugs/ingredients
  • Strong dehydrating agents → coagulation of the dispersion
  • Excess alcohol and electrolytes ✗ polymers

Sorbitol in one line

60% as sweet as sucrose, half the viscosity of simple syrup, no added preservative needed above 60% w/w, laxative in excess, 30% can be added to simple syrup to stop crystallisation.

Heat and inversion

Heat speeds sucrose dissolution but also inverts it — H⁺ acts as catalyst. Overheating caramelises sugar (amber colour) and makes the syrup more prone to fermentation and microbial growth.

No autoclaving

Syrups cannot be sterilised by autoclaving. Use boiled purified water, a permitted preservative, and store in a tight container.

Four preparation methods

  • Solution with heat
  • Solution by agitation without heat
  • Addition of sucrose to a medicated liquid
  • Percolation

Ipecac syrup

Prepared by percolation of powdered ipecac (emetine, cephaeline, psychotrine); glycerin and syrup are added. Used as an emetic, dose 15 mL.

Linctuses

High-sucrose viscous orals for bitter drugs, mainly for cough (soothes inflamed mucosa). Sugar-free versions replace sucrose with sorbitol.

Sweetener watch-outs

  • Cyclamate — banned in the USA
  • Aspartame — unstable when heated, PKU warning
  • Sucralose — stable across a broad pH range
  • Acesulfame-K — heat stable

Four ways to prepare a syrup

1 · Solution with heat

Sugar dissolved in water with heat; heat-stable extras added hot, then cooled. Volatile/heat-labile agents added after cooling.

2 · Agitation, no heat

Sugar dissolved by agitation alone in an oversized vessel — slower, but colourless and maximally stable.

3 · Sucrose + medicated liquid

Sucrose is added to an existing tincture/fluidextract or flavoured liquid (e.g. cherry, orange syrup).

4 · Percolation

Sucrose or the drug source is percolated; when a drug extractive is used, extraction and syrup-making are two separate steps (e.g. ipecac syrup).
